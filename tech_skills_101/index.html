Digital Explorers SIG

Tech Skills 101

I have taught several OLLI courses on digital technology. The primary aim of each of those courses was to help increase people's digital literacy (and so covered roughly the same set of topics). Each subsequent time I taught the course, I refined the syllabus and content based on student feedback from the previous course. This subsection contains the latest version of the course outline.

We drew from this outline for a number of meetings in the first half of 2026. In fact, in July we started repeating the course content from the beginning at group member's request.

Note:

  • The outline below is top-level. If/when members of the group request additional details, I will extend the content of this page.
  • I use the word "computer" here to collectively refer to desktop computers, laptops, smartphones, and tablets.

Syllabus

Part 1: Hardware

Primary aim: To provide you with a basic and practical understanding of key components inside computers, the role/purpose of those components, and how they work.

Key Subtopics
  • Processors, Memory, and Storage
  • Interactivity: components that allow you to interact with your computer
  • Connectivity: components that allow you to use other types of digital devices with your computer
  • Data: how information is represented "inside" a computer
  • Sizing: how the processing power and capacity of computers is measured
  • When purchasing a computer, determining how much processing power/capacity you need

Part 2: Software

Primary aim: Software is the aspect of digital technology that animates hardware. In my experience, when a person is struggling to do something with their computer or fix a problem they have encountered, the solution pertains to software more often than hardware. This part of the course is intended to increase your understanding of what software is and how it works in "layman's terms".

Key Subtopics
  • What is software
  • Types of software and how to identify them
  • Code: How software is created
  • Runtime Lifecycle: what an app does from the time you start it until you stop (i.e., "quit") it
  • Support Lifecycle:
  • Installing and uninstalling software
  • Operating Systems

    While there are many different operating systems on the market, they all serve the same purpose. This section explains the core functions/responsibilities of operating systems and the relationship between your computer's operating system and the apps you use.

  • User Interface

    What you see on your computer's screen at any moment in time includes visual elements you interact with to make your computer do things. Being able to identify those visual elements and know what they do is central to being able to effectively use your computer. I affectionately refer to this skill as "reading the screen." This section of the course provides a survey common visual elements (e.g., buttons, textboxes, menus, etc.), their purpose and use,

  • The File System

    Files are an essential aspect of the digital systems. The objective of this section is to provide you with an understanding of what files and folders are and the operating system's file system organizes them in storage.

  • Settings

    Computers are highly customizable (to a degree that they are somewhat like fingerprints: no two are completely alike. System settings provide the mechanism for customizing a computer. Contemporary operating systems tend to have several hundred settings. This section gives an overview of the categories and types of system settings found on most operating systems and describes some of the "pitfalls" you may encounter if/when settings change (which can occur for a variety reasons.)

Part 3: The Internet and The Cloud

Primary aim: The Internet and the Cloud have become fairly tightly coupled with most of the software we use on our computers. The part of the course discusses the global infrastructure (literally across the entire planet) that comprises Internet and the Cloud. To help solidify your understanding, this part includes sections that describe what happens "under the covers" when you undertake some common activities (e.g., visiting a site on the internet, sending/receiving email, saving a file to a cloud drive).

Part 4: Common Apps

There are certain things everyone does with their computer at some point: send/read email, visit sites on the internet, answer questions/explore topics of interest using Google Search (or other internet search engines). The sections in this part of the course aim to enhance/improve your skills and abilities using common apps. Below is a list of common kinds of apps. In my experience working with OLLI folks, most people are interested in the first five. There has generally been less interest in spending time discussing the other items on the list; I am including them nonetheless to create awareness that they are "on the menu" if/when members of the group wish to explore them.

  1. File managers
  2. Backup utilities
  3. Browsers
  4. Email
  5. Search and Find
  6. Text, Messaging, and Video Conferencing apps
  7. Word Processors
  8. Spreadsheets
  9. Presentation Apps

Part 5: Security & Privacy

Primary aim: To review the kinds of bad things that (unfortunately) can happen when using digital technology and introduce you to the kinds of tools and strategies that help you protect yourself from scams, hacks, and loss of privacy.

Key Subtopics
  • Survey of different forms of signing into accounts: two-factor/multi-factor authentication (2FA/MFA), passkeys, social sign-on
  • Password managers: why and how to use them; which ones to use and which ones to avoid
  • When and how to secure files you store in the cloud
  • Fingerprints and Footprints: how companies identify us online and track our activity
  • Risks of using public Wi-Fi and how to protect yourself
  • How to identify scam emails and text messages
