Digital Explorers SIG

Site Overview

This page's aim is to give you a top level introduction to what kind of information is on this site, how it is arranged, and how you get to the site's other pages. I add new content to the site on a routine basis and sometimes revise/update existing content.

I created this site when we originally formed the SIG in January 2026. My primary objective at that time was to provide a single place online that group members can visit to access presentations I prepared to help me facilitate discussions in our meetings. Some people like to have a copy of those materials during our meetings; they may want to look at them on a device they bring with them to meetings, or they may want to print them on paper that they bring to meetings to jot down notes of their own during our discussions.

During the initial month's of the SIG's existence, comments and questions others shared in our meetings gave me ideas for additional kinds of reference materials that folks might find useful as well as different ways to organize the information on the site to (hopefully) make easier to find content on the site.

The point being, the site changes with some regularity: I add new content, I revise existing content, and I sometimes (less often) reorganize the content. When I add new content, I try to faithfully/consistently let members aware (usually via email; sometimes also at the next meeting.)

Subsections

The content on this site is organized into several subsections:

  • Meeting schedule

    Lists meeting dates and topics. Links to presentations slides and handouts for each meeting can be accessed from this page.

  • Tech Skills 101

    This subsection contains the syllabus for the course I taught in the Fall 2025 term titled "Tech Skills 101: Driver's Ed for the Digital World". The initial SIG meetings held in the first half of 2026 stemmed from material covered in the "Tech Skills 101" course.

  • Social Impacts

    It may go without saying, but digital technology broadly impacts society, culture, and politics. We might find it interesting to discuss some of these impacts in future meetings. This subsection lists some digital technologies impacting society in significant and at times controversial ways. We could have some interesting group discussions on these topics if the group is interested.

  • Handbook

    Participants in the Fall 2025 OLLI course expressed a strong interest in having a digital technology "manual." The intent of this subsection is to assemble handout materials shared in SIG meetings into handbook that resembles what I think members of the fall course were interested in.

  • Terms and acronyms

    As in any field/discipline, there are a plethora of terms and acronyms associated with digital technology. This subsection's aim is to provide an inventory of such terms and acronyms that are frequently appear in articles, help/user guides, and discussions related to computers and software.

Navigation Menu

You access the primary page for the site's sections using the navigation menu. To see this menu click/tap on the icon in the upper right hand corner of the screen. The navigation menu will appear in a panel on the left hand side of your browser window. Click/tap on any of the items in the menu to access the associated subsection.

Each item in the menu has an icon and a label. If you use a smartphone to view the site, you may only see the subsection icons but not their labels. If this is the case, try turning your phone from a vertical (i.e., "portrait") orientation to a horizontal (i.e. "landscape") orientation. This should allow you to see the subsection labels as well as the icons.

Clicking/tapping on the "Home" item will return you to the site's main page. To close the navigation menu, click/tap on the X icon at the top of the panel.
